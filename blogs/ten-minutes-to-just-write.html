THE CRAFT

What happens when you give a child ten minutes to just write?

Anuradha · 2 min read

What will a child left alone with a blank page for ten minutes write? No marks, no word limit, no prescribed ending. Just ten minutes and a blank page. Most probably they will write about snacks, dreams and cracks in the ceiling. This is not a distraction. This is the point.

Try it at home this week. Hand your child a notebook. Set a timer. Do not correct spelling. Do not ask what it is about. Do not read it unless they offer.

The results are rarely tidy. A child may write three lines about a dream, then abandon it for a list of favourite snacks. Another may describe the crack in the ceiling in more detail than seems necessary. This is not a failure of the exercise. It is the exercise working.

Children who write without an audience write more honestly.

They notice things adults have stopped noticing: the sound a fan makes, the way a sibling argues, the exact shade of a school bag. Observation like this is not taught. It is practised, quietly, in the absence of pressure.

Confidence follows the same pattern. A child who is never marked learns that writing is a place to think, not a test to pass. That distinction shapes how they approach every essay, email and exam answer that comes later.

It requires ten minutes, a timer and the discipline to stay out of the way.

Space, held open long enough

This is the idea behind Joy of Writing. At first it is not about talent or technique. It is about the space — held open long enough for a child to fill it on their own terms. One story a week, and a real reply from Anuradha to every one.
